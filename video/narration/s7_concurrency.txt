And now the real test: twenty agents, one hundred intents, all at once. Each agent owns its own part of the service, with occasional writes to shared infrastructure — the genuinely contentious stuff. Thirty-plus intents per second. Zero errors. Zero lost intents. In the realistic run, every single intent folds — no negotiations needed. And under adversarial contention? Every conflict gets a recorded negotiation with a decision trail. Verified winners fold automatically. The rest wait for a human — with full context. Then rebase and retry: as the trunk advances, pending intents are re-checked, and everything that can fold, folds. This is what coordination looks like when it's designed for agents, not adapted from human workflows.
